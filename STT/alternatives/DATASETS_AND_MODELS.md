# Public human-speech datasets and small parameter models

Research checked 19 September 2026. Priority reflects this project's needs: numbers/percentages, assistant commands, music names and changing device aliases. Published benchmark scores are not measurements of this implementation. The accompanying report contains the actual from-scratch results.

## Dataset shortlist

| Dataset and primary source | Fit for this project | Current acquisition/use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [SLURP](https://github.com/pswietojanski/slurp) | Best available local source of natural assistant requests with artist, song and house/place spans. Multiple intents and microphones permit realistic confusion tests. | Original **SLURP-real** recordings collated; synthetic SLURP excluded. Prefer one headset recording per paired utterance. Sparse individual names remain a limitation. |
| [Snips/Sonos SmartLights and SmartSpeaker](https://github.com/sonos/spoken-language-understanding-research-datasets) | SmartLights supplies connected brightness commands; SmartSpeaker is especially relevant to music/artist/title requests. | Existing SmartLights close-microphone recordings reused. SmartSpeaker access currently requires the publisher's form; no form submitted and no claim that its audio was downloaded. Research/noncommercial terms are documented by the publisher. |
| [Fluent Speech Commands](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03670), [publisher distribution/license group](https://groups.google.com/a/fluent.ai/g/fluent-speech-commands) | Fixed action/object/location commands; useful for VCM and room/background examples. Limited slot ontology, not arbitrary Spotify names. | Existing local recordings reused. Academic research license; original web download endpoint is not reliably reachable. |
| [Google Speech Commands](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.03209), [official dataset catalog](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/speech_commands) | Strong fixed-keyword baseline; digits and direction words. Does not cover all English number phrases or personal entity names. | Existing 12-word subset, up to 300 files/word, collated. This is not the complete 35-word benchmark. |
| [Multilingual Spoken Words Corpus / MSWC](https://mlcommons.org/datasets/multilingual-spoken-words/) | Large vocabulary of original Common Voice word crops; good keyword pretraining and phonetic coverage. Alignment/crop quality needs filtering. | Existing pinned English numeric/context-word downloads reused, including brightness and percent. Full English corpus is tens of GB; full multilingual corpus is much larger. CC BY 4.0. |
| [Free Spoken Digit Dataset](https://github.com/Jakobovski/free-spoken-digit-dataset) | Convenient human 0–9 sanity check: 3,000 recordings but only six speakers. | Full local set used with 4/1/1 speakers for train/dev/test. It cannot establish 0–100 connected-command reliability. |
| [MLEnd Spoken Numerals](https://mlenddatasets.github.io/spoken_numerals/) | Covers elementary number words beyond digits, including teens/tens/hundred and varied intonation. | Previously downloaded 1,709 clips; 1,708 survive this duration protocol. Targets are canonical numeric categories, not independently verified verbatim transcripts. Local source snapshot lacks a clear license file; preserve that uncertainty. |
| [LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/12/) | Best straightforward next source for broad English phonetic/character training from scratch. Natural read speech with official speaker splits; different domain from home commands. | Existing local subset considered; 28 short recordings survive this bounded sample and 8-second limit. The 100-hour training archive is 6.3 GB; it was not newly downloaded in full. CC BY 4.0. |
| [LibriPhrase](https://github.com/gusrud1103/LibriPhrase), [author paper](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/shin22_interspeech.pdf) | Particularly useful next corpus for open-vocabulary KWS: natural LibriSpeech phrases, alignment metadata, easy and confusable negative pairs. | Surveyed, not trained here. Respect its designated training/evaluation partitions; do not turn its published test pairs into training examples. |
| [Common Voice](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/common-voice/platform-and-dataset/), [current publisher distribution](https://commonvoice.mozilla.org/en/datasets) | Accent/speaker variety and ordinary English for phonetic training. Text needs normalization and speaker/duplicate controls. | Surveyed directly; this experiment uses its existing MSWC word derivatives. Current releases are distributed through Mozilla Data Collective. |
| [GigaSpeech](https://github.com/SpeechColab/GigaSpeech) | More varied spoken English and hard background speech; official 10-hour XS subset offers a bounded next experiment. | Surveyed, not downloaded. Publisher access process includes a form. Audio/video licensing is separate from repository code licensing. |
| [The People's Speech](https://github.com/mlcommons/peoples-speech), [publisher description](https://mlcommons.org/2023/01/the-peoples-speech-dataset-just-got-better-dynamic-data-in-action/) | Large transcribed English pool for acoustic pretraining and negatives; much less targeted to device commands. | Surveyed, not downloaded; 30,000+ hours is unnecessary for the first small feasibility study. CC BY / CC BY-SA subsets require retaining provenance. |
| [Multilingual LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/94/) | Read English/other languages with limited-supervision split recipes. Useful if multilingual support becomes a goal. | Surveyed, not downloaded. The full English distribution is hundreds of GB even in Opus; low priority here. CC BY 4.0. |
| [LibriHeavy](https://github.com/k2-fsa/libriheavy) | Large English transcription corpus with punctuation/casing; potential broad-data extension when a compact recognizer is justified. | Surveyed, not downloaded; much larger than the name/number pilot needs. |
| [LibriTTS](https://www.openslr.org/60/) | Original human audiobook recordings can support pronunciation/phoneme work. A corpus intended for TTS training is not automatically synthetic audio. | Surveyed, not downloaded; deduplicate against LibriSpeech if combined. |
| [OHF Voice datasets](https://github.com/OHF-Voice/voice-datasets) | Real recordings released for voice synthesis training; useful supplementary speech, but limited speaker diversity for recognition evaluation. | Surveyed, not downloaded. These are human source recordings, not generated TTS. |
| [SpokeN-100](https://zenodo.org/records/10810044) | Superficially attractive 0–99 coverage, but the recordings are generated with ElevenLabs. | **Excluded** under the user's no-synthetic-data requirement. Metadata in older research is not evidence of human speech. Future synthetic roadmap only. |

The actual collated dataset has eight human sources, whose exact split counts and file hashes are in `data/parameter-study/audit.json` and `manifest.jsonl`. This run reused those existing public downloads and freshly downloaded the pinned [CMU pronunciation dictionary](https://github.com/cmusphinx/cmudict); its revision, hashes and license are in `sources/receipt.json`. A pronunciation dictionary contains text, not acoustic model weights.

## Models and approaches

| Approach | Primary model references | Judgment for this use case |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fixed KWS / direct parameter classification** | [DS-CNN / Hello Edge](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07128), [TC-ResNet](https://github.com/hyperconnect/TC-ResNet), [BC-ResNet](https://github.com/Qualcomm-AI-research/bcresnet), [MatchboxNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.08531) | Most appropriate for a small stable keyword set. VCM can incorporate such finite heads. Every new output class needs acoustic examples and retraining; a word list alone is insufficient. Our tested temporal depthwise residual classifier is an independent small baseline, not an exact reproduction of all four architectures. |
| **Audio enrollment / metric KWS** | [Few-Shot KWS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.14463), [open-set custom KWS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02161), [D-ProtoNet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13691) | A new name can be represented by a few real recordings. Suitable for user-enrolled device aliases without full transcription. Text-only playlist expansion is insufficient. Our separate randomly initialized encoder uses classification plus supervised contrastive training; enrollment averages normalized embeddings. |
| **Phonetic open-vocabulary KWS** | [CTC open-vocabulary KWS](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2016/zhuang16_interspeech.html), [CTC-aligned audio/text KWS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07923) | Best conceptual match for automatic text-list changes with a small acoustic model. Needs adequate phonetic training, pronunciations for proper names and strong unknown/homophone handling. Our tested baseline uses greedy phoneme CTC and substring edit matching. It does not reproduce the learned audio/text architecture or its published results. |
| **Compact character CTC STT** | [CTC original paper](https://www.cs.toronto.edu/~graves/icml_2006.pdf) | Flexible spelling and catalogs, but more data-dependent than a fixed keyword head. Tested as the allowed, discouraged baseline using a newly initialized compact CNN/BiGRU. It does not load or continue the retired convolutional ASR checkpoint. |
| **Grammar-constrained speech-to-intent / speech-to-phrase** | [OHF Speech-to-Phrase](https://github.com/OHF-voice/speech-to-phrase), [Home Assistant local voice guidance](https://www.home-assistant.io/voice_control/voice_remote_local_assistant), [HA sentence templates](https://developers.home-assistant.io/docs/voice/intent-recognition/template-sentence-syntax/) | Strong architecture direction for bounded home controls: compile command templates, number ranges and entity aliases into a grammar. The existing OHF package downloads acoustic models, so it is **not a from-scratch candidate trained in this run**. Its approach remains speech recognition even though it constrains the output space. A future compact phonetic reader could use the same grammar idea. |
| **Learned audio/text keyword retrieval** | [LibriPhrase / audio-text agreement](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/shin22_interspeech.pdf), [MM-KWS](https://github.com/aizhiqi-work/MM-KWS) | Promising successor to greedy phoneme matching for large changing lists. Requires phrase-pair training and hard negatives; review any synthetic-data branches before use. Surveyed only here. |

## Recommended next data order

1. Add **real connected brightness/volume recordings** spanning all 101 values, optional percent, out-of-range/multiple-number negatives, multiple microphones and unseen speakers. Current isolated-word data does not fill this gap.
2. Expand phonetic/keyword representation training with **MSWC and LibriPhrase**, plus a bounded LibriSpeech train-clean subset. Preserve speaker and source-audio separation across derivatives.
3. Use SLURP music/entity examples and, if available, the publisher's SmartSpeaker corpus for names in natural commands. Reserve whole entity names and speakers for the expansion test.
4. Collect real audio enrollment for chosen Home Assistant aliases. No Spotify/HA integration is needed to validate a local catalog fixture.
5. Later, test explicitly labeled TTS for rare names and connected numbers, with real-human evaluation kept untouched. TTS voices, templates and source components must not leak into the claimed real test. Synthetic-only 0–99 benchmarks cannot establish human command accuracy.
